Three things shipped this release, and all three came from the same
complaint: the front desk was doing work the system already had the
information to do.

## Instagram DMs book without leaving the thread

Until now, a DM conversation ended with a link. The client tapped out to a
booking page, and roughly a third of them never arrived — they got
distracted, the page asked for details they had already given, or they simply
did not want to fill in a form at eleven at night.

Aurelius now completes the booking inside the thread. It reads the diary,
offers the two or three slots that actually fit the treatment, holds the one
the client picks for ten minutes, and confirms. Deposits are taken in the
same conversation where the studio has them switched on.

- Works on Instagram and Facebook DMs today; WhatsApp is in beta
- Recognises returning clients from the handle, so nobody re-types a phone number
- Hands over to a human the moment the conversation stops being about booking

## The waitlist refills a cancellation in ninety seconds

The old waitlist was a list. Someone had to read it, decide who to call, and
call them — which meant it worked on quiet mornings and did nothing on the
days it mattered.

The new one is a queue with rules. When a slot opens, Aurelius works out who
can actually take it — right treatment, right practitioner, patch test valid,
not already booked that week — and offers it to the best match with a short
expiry. No answer, it moves down the list.

The median gap between a cancellation and a filled slot across the studios
running the beta was **ninety-four seconds**. The previous median, measured
on the same studios, was just over four hours.

## Patch-test rules live on the treatment

Patch-test policy used to live in the head of whoever had been there longest.
Now it is a property of the treatment: how long before the appointment, how
long it stays valid, and what happens when it has lapsed.

When a client books something that needs one, the flow adjusts on its own —
it offers the patch test first, blocks slots that are too close, and tells
the client why in a sentence they can act on rather than a policy paragraph.

## Smaller things

- Cancellation and late-cancellation messages now follow the studio's own policy wording
- The diary sync with Fresha handles a rescheduled appointment without creating a duplicate
- Consent exports include the practitioner who took them
- Reminder timing is per-treatment rather than per-studio

Everything above is on for every studio already. If you want a walkthrough of
the waitlist rules on your own diary, ask and we will sit with you for twenty
minutes.
