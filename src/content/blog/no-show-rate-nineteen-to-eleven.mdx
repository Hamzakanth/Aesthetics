---
title: How one clinic took its no-show rate from 19% to 11%
excerpt: No new staff, no price change, no shouting about policies. Just follow-up that happens on the days everyone is too busy to send it.
category: Case study

A three-room skin clinic, two practitioners and a part-time front desk,
running around 180 appointments a month. Before Aurelius, 19% of those
appointments were no-shows or cancellations inside the notice window. That is
roughly thirty-four wasted hours a month, most of it in the ninety-minute
treatment slots that were hardest to fill.

## What they had already tried

Not nothing. The owner had:

- A written 24-hour cancellation policy on the website
- A reminder text the day before, sent manually most days
- Deposits on the two most expensive treatments

The policy was rarely enforced, because enforcing it meant an awkward
conversation with a client the clinic wanted to keep. The reminders went out
on quiet days and got skipped on busy ones — which is to say they were
missing exactly when the diary was fullest.

## What changed

Three things, over about six weeks.

**Confirmations became a question with a deadline.** Every appointment had to
be confirmed, and unconfirmed ones were chased twice on their own schedule
rather than whenever someone remembered.

**Reminders were set per treatment.** Quick appointments got one reminder the
day before. The long, expensive ones got an earlier one at 72 hours — which
is when a client can still realistically move it rather than drop it.

**The policy was written down as rules.** Notice period, fee, and what
happens on a repeat. Once it was in the messages, the desk stopped having to
decide case by case, and clients stopped hearing three different versions.

They did not raise deposits, add staff, or change prices.

## The numbers after twelve weeks

| Metric | Before | After |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No-show and late-cancel rate | 19% | 11% |
| Unconfirmed at 24 hours | 21% | 5% |
| Cancelled slots refilled | 12% | 46% |
| Reminders actually sent | ~70% of days | every day |

The refill number is the one the owner cared about most. An 11% no-show rate
still means cancellations happen — but a cancellation that gets filled within
the hour is an inconvenience rather than a loss.

> "The change wasn't that we got stricter. It's that the same thing happens
> every time now, including on the days I'm double-booked and not thinking
> about it."

## What did not work

Two things worth reporting honestly.

Moving the reminder to 48 hours for *every* treatment made things slightly
worse for short appointments — people confirmed, then forgot. It went back to
24 hours for anything under an hour.

And the first version of the late-cancellation message quoted the fee too
early in the wording. It read as a threat. Rewriting it to lead with the
offer to move the appointment, and mention the fee second, recovered more
appointments than it lost.
