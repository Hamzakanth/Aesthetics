The standard retention advice is to text your client base every six weeks.
It is easy to implement, it produces a small bump, and it teaches your best
clients to ignore you.

The problem is that six weeks is not a fact about your clients. It is a fact
about your calendar.

## Treatments have their own clocks

A brow lamination wants roughly six to eight weeks. Gel infills want two to
three. A laser course has a protocol with fixed gaps, and skipping one costs
the client the result they paid for. Injectables sit somewhere between three
and six months depending on what and how much.

Send all of those people the same six-week message and you are early for
half of them and late for the rest. Early reads as pushy. Late means they
already went somewhere else — the appointment they needed happened in week
three, and you asked in week six.

## The three numbers that matter

For each treatment, you want:

1. **The ideal interval** — what the treatment actually needs to work.
2. **The ask window** — when a person is receptive, usually a few days before the interval closes.
3. **The lapse point** — the gap after which someone stops being a returning client and becomes a re-acquisition.

Most studios have the first number, in someone's head. Very few have written
down the second or third, which is why retention work turns into blanket
campaigns.

## Rebook at the chair first

Nothing beats the rebook that happens while the client is still sitting
down. If you do one thing from this post, make the rebook part of finishing
the appointment rather than part of leaving.

But you will not get everyone, and the people you miss are the ones the
interval logic is for.

> A follow-up sent on the right day for that treatment converts several
> times better than the same words sent on a schedule that suits your
> marketing calendar.

## What to do with a lapsed client

Once someone is past the lapse point, stop sending them rebooking messages —
they are not rebooking, they are being won back, and that is a different
conversation with a different tone. Blending the two is how a retention
programme ends up annoying both groups at once.

## How we approach it

Aurelius holds the interval on the treatment, not the client, then adjusts
per person based on the gaps they have actually kept. Someone who reliably
comes back at nine weeks gets asked at nine, not six. The message goes out
in the ask window with slots attached, and if they take one it books
without a conversation.

The point is not the automation. It is that the timing is a property of the
work you do, and it should be written down somewhere other than the most
experienced person's memory.
