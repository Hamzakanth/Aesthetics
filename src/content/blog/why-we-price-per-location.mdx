Per-seat pricing is the default in software, and for good reason — it is easy
to explain and it scales with the value the customer gets. For us it does
neither, so we do not use it.

## The problem with seats in a studio

A studio does not add front-desk headcount because it is growing. It adds
headcount because the messages have become unmanageable — thirty DMs a day,
an inbox nobody owns, and someone has to be paid to sit with it.

That is precisely the problem Aurelius exists to remove. Charging per seat
would mean the studio drowning in enquiries pays us the most, and the studio
we have successfully helped pays us less each time it works. Our revenue
would move in the opposite direction to our usefulness.

It also creates a small, daily disincentive to do the right thing: sharing a
login instead of giving the new therapist her own, or leaving the Saturday
junior off the system entirely. Both are worse for the studio's data and
worse for ours.

## What we charge for instead

One price per location. Every member of staff gets their own login, every
channel is included, and the price does not move when you have a busy month.

A second location is a second price, because a second location genuinely is a
second front desk with its own diary, its own staff and its own clients. That
is the unit where our costs actually scale too.

## What this means in practice

- Add as many staff accounts as you have staff
- Message volume is not metered, and a viral post does not produce an invoice
- The bill is the same in January as it is in December, which makes it a fixed cost you can plan around

## The honest tradeoff

Per-location pricing is worse for a very small studio — a single room with one
practitioner pays the same as a three-room clinic on the same plan, and per
appointment that is a higher number. We think that is the right compromise,
because the alternative is a pricing page with five metrics on it that nobody
can forecast.

If you are a single room and the number does not work, tell us. That
conversation has changed our pricing before.
